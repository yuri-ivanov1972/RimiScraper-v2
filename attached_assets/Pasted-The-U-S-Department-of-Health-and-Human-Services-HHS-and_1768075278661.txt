The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and the USDA recently released the 2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines for Americans, which for the first time explicitly recommend limiting ultra-processed foods (UPF). These guidelines define UPFs not just by their nutrient profile (high salt, sugar, fat) but by the presence of industrial additives that "mimic the sensory qualities of whole foods" or act as markers of industrial formulation.

According to the NOVA classification system—the gold standard used by researchers and referenced in modern health policy—these are the 100 most suspicious additives categorized by their function. These ingredients generally fail the "kitchen cupboard test" (you wouldn't find them in a home kitchen) and are considered markers of ultra-processing.

How to use this list:

If you see more than one of these ingredients on a label, the product is almost certainly NOVA Group 4 (Ultra-Processed).[8] The new dietary guidelines emphasize that the health impact of these foods is likely due to the "cocktail effect" of these additives working together to disrupt gut health and satiety signals.
