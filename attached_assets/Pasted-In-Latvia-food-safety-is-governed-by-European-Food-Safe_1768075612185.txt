In Latvia, food safety is governed by European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) regulations, which are generally stricter than US (FDA) standards. However, "Ultra-Processed Foods" (UPF) are defined not just by safety, but by their industrial nature.

To adapt this for a Latvian consumer, we must use E-numbers (the EU coding system) and account for the fact that some additives (like Titanium Dioxide or BVO) are already banned in the EU.

Here is the list of the 100 most suspicious additives found in Latvian supermarkets, categorized by the NOVA classification system (Group 4: Ultra-processed).

Latvian-Specific Advice:

Check the "Cīsiņi" and "Desas": In Latvia, look for the "Augstākā labuma" (Highest quality) label. If it doesn't have it, it likely contains E250, E450, and Mechanically Separated Meat.

Bread (Maize): Latvia has excellent rye bread (Rupjmaize). However, "Tostermaize" (toast bread) is almost always UPF, containing E282 and E471.

The "Lāču" or "Local" Rule: If a product contains ingredients that a Latvian grandmother wouldn't recognize (e.g., Sojas lecitīns, Glikozes sīrups), it is ultra-processed.

Titanium Dioxide (E171): You will no longer find this in Latvian food (banned in EU since 2022), whereas it is still common in US sweets. If you see it on an imported product, it is illegal in the EU.
